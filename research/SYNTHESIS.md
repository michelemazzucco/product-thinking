# Synthesis: how to interrogate a product idea

This file merges the 20 research notes into one structure for an interview skill. The skill asks hard questions. It does not cheer. Its goal is to show the difference between what the founder **knows** and what the founder **believes**. At the end, it gives a clear next step.

Source codes (for example `[MOESTA]`) are in the table in [README.md](README.md).

---

## 1. The core ideas behind the skill

The 20 sources agree on these points:

1. **Demand comes from a struggle in a specific context, not from a product.** "A struggling moment causes demand." [MOESTA] "If there's no struggle, there's no demand." [DSS] A job is "the progress that a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance." [CHRISTENSEN]
2. **Past behavior is evidence. Opinions are not.** "Bitchin' ain't switchin'." [MOESTA] "Ready to pay is different than paying." [WEINSTEIN] Feedback from friends counts as zero. [WEINSTEIN]
3. **Start from the problem, but know why it is possible now.** "No problem paragraph, no problem." [MCALLISTER] But a pure "find a problem, build a solution" idea is often a consensus idea. Big wins come from a new capability and the right "who". [RACHLEFF] [KRIEGER]
4. **Compare with the status quo, not with an ideal.** The current solution is also a function (email, a wall calendar, piracy, doing nothing). [SINGER] [EK] [CHRISTENSEN]
5. **Win a narrow group first.** Find the "Sarah", the "Nicole", the first segment, the wedge. [WEINSTEIN] [VOHRA] [KARRI] [EK] [GURLEY]
6. **The cheapest test of the riskiest assumption comes before the build.** [RIES] [CAGAN] [KRIEGER] [WEINSTEIN] [SHOWRUN]
7. **Decide in advance what result will make you stop.** [RIES] [SHREYAS] [CAGAN] [MCALLISTER]
8. **The real fit signal is pull.** Users "want to pull it out of your hands". There is organic growth. 40% of users would be "very disappointed" without the product. Users are angry when the product goes down. [RACHLEFF] [VOHRA] [WEINSTEIN]
9. **Value also needs a barrier.** Profit is competed away unless something stops copies. [NEUMANN] [HELMER] [GURLEY]
10. **Founder behavior is part of the idea.** Look for these warning signs: "I'll know it when I see it", work on the "sexy glamorous jobs" (brand, logo, polish), and side quests instead of problems one and two. [SHOWRUN] [KARRI] [WEINSTEIN] [SHREYAS]

## 2. Interview method (how the skill asks)

These rules come from how the sources say to interview customers. The skill uses the same rules on the founder.

- **Ask one question at a time, then wait.** Do not answer for the founder. [WEINSTEIN: "sit in silence"]
- **Ask for a specific past event, not a hypothetical one.** Ask "Tell me about the last time…" and "What happened the day before?" Do not ask "Would people…?" [MOESTA] [DSS] [WEINSTEIN]
- **Ask for more detail.** Use "Tell me more" and "Give me an example". Use a direct "why" several times only when you need the root cause (drill → hole → … → "I want to read better"). [MOESTA] [DSS]
- **Repeat answers back a little wrong.** Then the founder corrects you and adds detail. When the founder cannot find words, give two options: "Was it more X or more Y?" [MOESTA]
- **Ask what vague words mean.** If the founder says "easier", "convenient", "productive", "healthy" or "all-in-one", ask what that means in a real situation. [DSS] [CHRISTENSEN]
- **Do not let the founder pitch.** Ask them to describe the problem without the product. Then ask again without the technology. [MCALLISTER] [KRIEGER]
- **Classify each claim on the evidence ladder (section 3), and say the level.** For example: "This is stated intent, not behavior."
- **Keep a list of assumptions** while the interview goes on. Show it at the end.
- **Name red flags directly and calmly.** Do not lecture. Be "humble but confident" [KRIEGER]. A red flag is a question to answer, not a verdict.
- **Before deep questions, find the stage of the idea and the type of business** (section 5). Do not ask about outage anger if there are no users.
- **Depth is more important than coverage.** If one answer is weak, stay on it. Do not continue only to finish a checklist.

## 3. The evidence ladder

Use this to grade each claim. Most "validation" is at level 1–4. Real validation starts at level 5.

| Level | Evidence | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | The founder believes it | "You're not smart enough to instinctively know why people really buy." [DSS] |
| 1 | Compliments, "great idea", feedback from friends | Count as zero. [WEINSTEIN] |
| 2 | People complain about the problem | "Bitchin' ain't switchin'." [MOESTA] |
| 3 | Stated intent: "I'd use it", "I'd pay for that" | Surveys can contradict behavior (93% said they wanted Energy Star; all of them bought the finished basement). [MOESTA] |
| 4 | Sign-ups, waitlist, likes | Use them to find people, not as proof. [VOHRA] [KARRI] |
| 5 | A costly action that nobody asked for: a workaround they built, or a screenshot, video or bullet points they sent | People spend their own time. [CHRISTENSEN] [WEINSTEIN] |
| 6 | People recently switched to something to solve this (a competitor counts) | The best interview source. [MOESTA] [DSS] |
| 7 | People use a prototype, concierge or MVP again and again (≥2 times in 2 weeks) | [VOHRA] [RIES] |
| 8 | People paid real money (even $1, or a refundable $10k) | [WEINSTEIN] [KARRI pay-what-you-want slider] |
| 9 | Pull: ≥40% "very disappointed", anger at an outage, organic word-of-mouth growth | [VOHRA] [WEINSTEIN] [RACHLEFF] |

## 4. Validation dimensions and question bank

The order follows a natural interview sequence. Each dimension has: **the goal**, **the main questions** (always ask these), **deeper questions** (when an answer is weak), and **red flags**.

### D0. What is the idea, really?
**Goal:** a clear statement that a new hire can repeat. It is not a category or a list of features.
**Main questions**
- In one sentence, what changes for the customer? Do not name a feature. [CAGAN] [SINGER]
- Where did this idea come from? Was it a customer problem, your own itch, a technology you wanted to use, or one big customer who asked for it? [CAGAN] [MCALLISTER] [KRIEGER]
- What will this product explicitly *not* do? [GIL] [SHOWRUN]

**Deeper questions**
- If I show you three mockups, can you say which one fits, and why? Or will you "know it when you see it"? [SHOWRUN]
- Are you putting "ingredients in the pantry" together (a model, an API, a dataset you have) and then looking for someone to feed? [MCALLISTER]
- If you describe the company by the outcome it creates, not by the mechanism, what is the market? [COLLISON]

**Red flags:** a category label ("an AI calendar", "a collaboration tool"); a list of features; "I'll know it when I see it"; the solution came first and the problem was added later.

### D1. The problem and the struggling moment
**Goal:** a real, specific moment when a real person struggles, and proof that it causes action.
**Main questions**
- Write the problem paragraph of your press release. Do not mention your solution. Does it work alone? [MCALLISTER]
- Tell me about the last time a real person had this struggle. What day was it? What happened just before? What did they do next? [MOESTA] [DSS]
- Is this their *first* problem, or their fifth? "People don't really get out of bed for their second problem." [WEINSTEIN]
- What progress does the person want to make: functional, social and emotional? [CHRISTENSEN] [KRIEGER] [DSS]

**Deeper questions**
- What makes today "the day" that they look for something new? [MOESTA]
- How long has this struggle existed? Why has nobody solved it? [MOESTA]
- Describe x (the situation before) and y (the situation after) in the customer's words. Would they pay to go from x to y? [SINGER]
- Is anyone building a workaround now with spreadsheets, scripts or tape? Show me one. [CHRISTENSEN] [KRIEGER]
- If nobody tries to solve this problem today, why do you think people want it solved? [KRIEGER]

**Red flags:** the founder imagined the problem in a room [MOESTA]; the job is an adjective ("convenience"); complaints but no action; a vitamin, not a painkiller [MCALLISTER].

### D2. The customer: one specific person, then one segment
**Goal:** a person, not a demographic group, and a first segment narrow enough to win.
**Main questions**
- Describe your customer at the "Sarah" level. What is her role? What tabs does she have open? What must she solve by 4 PM today? [WEINSTEIN]
- Who is your high-expectation customer: the most demanding person who gets the main benefit and tells others? [VOHRA]
- Which single segment do you want to be the default choice for first? Who is explicitly *not* a fit yet? [KARRI] [EK]

**Deeper questions**
- Did you define the customer by demographics or by circumstance? What proves that the circumstance causes the purchase? [CHRISTENSEN] [DSS]
- In B2B, who uses the product, and who spends their personal reputation to buy it? [DSS]
- Which customer do the incumbents refuse to serve or fail to serve, and why? [RACHLEFF]
- If this "who" does not care, what other "who" could want the same product? [RACHLEFF]
- Is this your own itch? If yes, what evidence shows that other people share it? [KRIEGER] [KARRI] [DSS]

**Red flags:** "SMBs", "millennials", "everyone who…"; designing for everyone [KARRI]; trying to serve the enterprise market on day one.

### D3. Today's alternative and the forces of switching
**Goal:** a clear picture of what the customer must stop using to use you, and why they will switch.
**Main questions**
- What does your customer use for this today? Include "nothing", a workaround, or "ask a friend". [CHRISTENSEN] [SINGER]
- What must they stop using to start using you? [CHRISTENSEN] [DSS]
- **Push:** what makes them leave the current way? Is it strong and frequent? [MOESTA]
- **Pull:** what attracts them? Is it connected to a push they already feel? [MOESTA]
- **Anxiety:** what will make them afraid to switch (cost, learning, data migration, looking stupid)? [MOESTA]
- **Habit:** what do they love about the old way? [MOESTA]

**Deeper questions**
- Look at the problem from the customer's moment, not from your category. What is a surprising competitor (the "run" that competes with a Milky Way)? [MOESTA] [DSS]
- If you compare with the status quo, not with your ideal, how much better is the result? [SINGER]
- Are you asking people to pay for something they expect to get free? [EK]
- Is it a better version of something people already buy, or a new category that must "cross the chasm"? [RACHLEFF]
- Which friction can you remove instead of adding features? [MOESTA] [DSS]

**Red flags:** "we have no competitors"; the competitor list has only products in the same category; the plan is "more features" and ignores anxiety and habit.

### D4. Evidence: what have people actually done?
**Goal:** move every important claim up the evidence ladder.
**Main questions**
- Name three people who recently switched to a solution like yours, or to a competitor. Tell me the story of each one, from the first thought to the first use. [MOESTA]
- How much of your positive feedback comes from friends or people who like you? What is left if you count it as zero? [WEINSTEIN]
- Which evidence is people *saying* something, and which evidence is people *doing* something? [MOESTA] [DSS]
- *(If there are users)* If the product disappeared tomorrow, what percentage would be "very disappointed"? Did you ask? [VOHRA]
- *(If there are users)* If the product went down for 20 minutes, who would call you? [WEINSTEIN]

**Deeper questions**
- When you talk to prospects, do you pitch first? What did they say before you mentioned the product? [WEINSTEIN]
- Who are the five to ten target customers that you can send a text message to today? [WEINSTEIN]
- Is any growth organic word of mouth? Is it growing? [RACHLEFF]
- Do you use vanity metrics (totals) or actionable metrics (cohorts, per customer)? [RIES]

**Red flags:** validation = waitlist and compliments; no interviews with switchers; users do not care when the product breaks.

### D5. Value, money and business viability
**Goal:** proof that someone will pay, and a model that works for the business, not only for the user.
**Main questions**
- Has anyone paid, even $1? If not, what stops you from asking today? [WEINSTEIN]
- If you told a prospect "it's $10,000, fully refundable", what would they say it must do? [WEINSTEIN]
- How will this make money, and why that price? [GIL] [CAGAN]
- Is your innovation that you make the same thing cheaper, or that you make something better that people pay more for? [NEUMANN]

**Deeper questions**
- What does it cost to get a customer, and what will they pay over time? [CAGAN] [RIES]
- Do legal, compliance, partner or platform rules block or change this? [CAGAN]
- What kind of revenue is it? How would a skeptic value one dollar of it? [GURLEY]
- Is it a retention business or a transactional business? How does that change what you invest in? [KARRI]
- Are you building a job for yourself, or a company that will earn above-normal profit? Both are fine, but they need different plans. [NEUMANN]

**Red flags:** you checked only value risk and not viability risk [CAGAN]; "they said they would pay"; custom deals that you cannot repeat [RIES]; a big partner that wants features but will not pay in advance [WEINSTEIN].

### D6. Why now, and why you?
**Goal:** a real change that makes this possible now, and a reason this founder is the right person.
**Main questions**
- What changed (a technology, a behavior, a rule) that makes this possible or necessary now and not five years ago? [CAGAN] [KRIEGER] [RACHLEFF]
- Is the wave arriving now, or are you too early or too late? [KRIEGER]
- Why are you the right person to do this? What do you have that others do not? [CAGAN] [MCALLISTER]

**Deeper questions**
- Name the problem without the technology. Are you a technology that looks for a problem? [KRIEGER]
- Of all the ideas you could work on, is this the *best* use of your next year, or only a *good* one? [SHREYAS]
- Who is the specific person with the vision? Can you explain it so that a skeptic cares? [COLLISON]

**Red flags:** "AI makes it possible" and no named problem; no reason why the founder in particular should do it.

### D7. Distribution and growth
**Goal:** a believable way for the first 100 customers to find you, which fits how they choose solutions today.
**Main questions**
- How will the next 100 customers find you? Why do you believe this? [RIES]
- How do your customers find solutions like yours today (friends, search, an advisor)? Does your plan match that? [WEINSTEIN]
- Does each use of the product bring new users (for example, a share that links back)? Does that channel still work today? [KRIEGER]

**Deeper questions**
- If you stopped all outreach tomorrow, would people still find you? [RACHLEFF]
- If it is a new consumer app, how will people find it, when most people install about one new app a year? Could it be a feature inside a product that they already use? [EK]
- Are you spending money on growth before you have proof that people love the product? [RACHLEFF] [VOHRA]
- After you have users, what is the second product that you can sell through the same channel? [GIL]

**Red flags:** "a good product will spread by itself" [GIL]; paid growth that never becomes organic [GURLEY]; a growth playbook copied from another time.

### D8. Differentiation, moat and non-consensus
**Goal:** a benefit plus a barrier, and an honest answer to "what if a big company copies this?"
**Main questions**
- Why can't the biggest incumbent add this as a feature next quarter? What would they have to give up to copy you? [HELMER]
- What is the benefit (lower cost or higher price), and what is the barrier that keeps it in five years? [HELMER] [NEUMANN]
- What do you believe about this market that most smart people would disagree with? [RACHLEFF]

**Deeper questions**
- For each part of your moat: is it a castle or a shack? [HELMER]
- If a rich competitor gave a team a billion dollars to copy you, how far would the money get them? [COLLISON]
- If you say you have a network effect: does your 10,000th customer get clearly more value than your 1,000th? [GURLEY] [HELMER]
- If you say you have a data advantage: at what point does more data stop improving the product? [HELMER]
- If you are right, why has nobody done this? [RACHLEFF]
- Are you copying a method from another company without the system that made it work there? [EK]

**Red flags:** the moat is "team", "speed" or "execution" [HELMER]; a "flywheel" that has no material effect; "network effects" without a mechanism [GURLEY]; right, but a consensus idea [RACHLEFF]; stuck in the middle, neither a niche nor real scale [EK].

### D9. The riskiest assumption and the smallest test
**Goal:** one assumption that kills the idea if it is wrong, and a test of it this week that costs almost nothing.
**Main questions**
- What are the two or three assumptions that kill this idea if they are wrong? Which one is the riskiest? [RIES]
- Which of the four risks is largest: value, usability, feasibility or viability? [CAGAN]
- What is the cheapest thing that you can show to real customers this week to test that assumption? [RIES] [SHOWRUN]

**Deeper questions**
- Can you deliver the result by hand (concierge), or fake the automation, before you write code? [RIES]
- What is the simplest version, the "whiteboard ad system" or the "Dot Grid"? [KRIEGER] [SINGER]
- What is the smallest "proof of existence" that the hardest part is possible? [WEINSTEIN]
- If the test gives a different result than you expect, does it seriously change what you think? If not, test something bigger. [RIES]
- Is the plan to learn with prototypes, or to learn by shipping full releases? [CAGAN]

**Red flags:** months of building before a customer sees anything; tests that cannot fail; building the scalable system before the simple version.

### D10. Pre-mortem and kill criteria
**Goal:** an honest failure story, and stop conditions written before the test.
**Main questions**
- It is six months after launch, and the product failed badly. Write the story. What is the first cause? [SHREYAS]
- **Tiger:** what can actually kill this? **Elephant:** what assumption does nobody say aloud? **Paper tiger:** what will other people worry about that is not important? [SHREYAS]
- What result will make you stop or pivot? Write it down now. [RIES] [MCALLISTER]
- If you must write "go", "no-go" or "not yet" today, which one do you write? What would change your answer? [CAGAN]

**Deeper questions**
- How many cycles of "modest results" will you accept before you question the problem and not the execution? [RIES]
- If you must pivot, what do you keep: the segment, the problem, or one feature that people already use? [RIES] [KRIEGER]
- What bad news about this idea do your advisors already know that you have not said? [SHOWRUN]

**Red flags:** no stop condition; the "living dead" pattern (more features, flat results) or the "compulsive jumper" pattern [RIES]; bad news is hidden.

### D11. Focus and founder behavior
**Goal:** the founder works on problems one and two, not on side quests.
**Main questions**
- What are your problems one and two now? How much of last week went to them? [WEINSTEIN]
- How much time went to the "sexy glamorous jobs" (brand, logo, tools, polish) and how much to the core problem? [SHOWRUN] [KARRI]
- Which hard task do you keep postponing (customer calls, pricing, strategy)? Why? [SHREYAS]

**Deeper questions**
- What can you tell early customers "not yet" without losing them? [KARRI]
- Are you waiting for better conditions (funding, a co-founder, a tool)? What would a high-agency version of you do now? [SHREYAS]
- Which of your ideas do you defend because you are attached to them, and not because of evidence? [SHOWRUN]

**Red flags:** optics (announcements, buzz) are more important than results [SHREYAS]; "I need more time"; you polish what people do not need yet ("craft is dessert" [WEINSTEIN]).

## 5. Adapt to the stage and type of the idea

Ask these first. They decide which questions are relevant.

**Stage**
| Stage | Focus | Skip or change |
|---|---|---|
| Idea only | D0–D3, D6, D9, D10 | D4 questions about the 40% test and outages: ask "what will you measure?" instead |
| Prototype / a few testers | D1–D4, D9, D10 | — |
| Users, no revenue | D4, D5, D7, D8 | Make D1 short if the switch stories are already strong |
| Revenue | D4 (pull), D5 (unit economics), D7, D8 | — |

**Type (extra modules)**
- **Marketplace / network:** Gurley's tests: value against penetration; fragmented supply; customers who use many providers, not one; how often people use it; cold start; how good liquidity is in one small area; lead-gen "fool's gold". Ek's supply questions: what do suppliers earn, and why would they give up 99% of their market? [GURLEY] [EK]
- **B2B:** who buys and who uses; the person who spends their reputation to buy [DSS]; self-serve or sales [COLLISON]; "talk up to the user" [COLLISON]; a big partner that wants features without payment [WEINSTEIN].
- **Consumer:** organic exponential growth as the test of fit [RACHLEFF]; a feature or a standalone app [EK]; a growth loop inside the product [KRIEGER].
- **Lifestyle / indie vs. venture-scale:** which kind it is, and if the plan fits that kind. [NEUMANN] [EK: niche or scale]

## 6. Where the sources disagree (the skill must handle this)

| Tension | Side A | Side B | How the skill handles it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem first or capability first | Problem first: "no problem paragraph, no problem". [MCALLISTER] [CAGAN] [KRIEGER] | A pure problem-first idea is often a consensus idea. Start from a technology change, then ask "who cares?" [RACHLEFF] | Ask both: "What changed that makes this possible?" and "Say the problem without the technology." |
| Metrics or taste | No metrics for each feature and no A/B tests. Success = customers say the problem is solved. [KARRI] | Clear metrics for customer value, cohorts, the 40% score. [WEINSTEIN] [VOHRA] [RIES] | Accept either one only if it measures *customer value*. Reject vanity metrics, and reject taste with no customer contact. |
| Strategy early or later | Think about power from the start. Counter-positioning is necessary. [HELMER] | Be very practical. Do unscalable things. Solve today's problem. [KRIEGER] [GURLEY] | Before fit, ask only the incumbent and counter-positioning question. Ask the full moat questions later. |
| Narrow or big | Narrow wedge, one segment, a small group that wants it a lot. [VOHRA] [KARRI] [EK] [GURLEY] | Think big. Use a broad frame. Ideas under $100M are not worth discussion. [SHREYAS] [MCALLISTER] [COLLISON] | A narrow start and a large frame. Ask for both. Challenge whichever one is missing. |
| Own itch or humility | Scratch your own itch. Lived experience. [KRIEGER] [KARRI] | "You're not smart enough to know why people buy." Hypotheses made in a room are "100% wrong". [DSS] [MOESTA] | Your own itch is a good start. It does not prove that other people have it. Ask for evidence from people who are not the founder. |
| Surveys | The "very disappointed" survey works. [VOHRA] | Surveys contradict behavior. [MOESTA] | The Superhuman survey asks *active users about real use*. Do not trust surveys about future intent. |

## 7. How the session ends (outputs the skill should produce)

1. **The idea in one sentence**, as the founder can now say it.
2. **Known and believed:** each key claim with its level on the evidence ladder.
3. **Assumption list**, ranked by risk. The top one is marked.
4. **The red flags that came up**, each with the dimension and the source.
5. **Pre-mortem summary:** tiger, elephant, paper tigers.
6. **The test this week:** the cheapest test of the riskiest assumption, with a pass/fail threshold written in advance.
7. **Kill criteria:** what result will stop the idea or cause a pivot.
8. **Verdict:** go / not yet / no-go, with the single reason that has the most weight. [CAGAN]

---

## 8. Open questions for the skill design

- **Tone:** a hard investor interview, a coaching session, or a mode that the user selects?
- **Length:** one long session, or one session for each dimension that you can continue later? (D0–D11 have about 150 questions. One session cannot ask all of them.)
- **Memory:** save each idea's interview as a file, so a later session can check if the evidence improved?
- **Output:** a markdown "idea dossier" in the project?
