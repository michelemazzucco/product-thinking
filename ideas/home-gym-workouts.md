## Original pitch
> i want to create a software that, based on a picture of your home gym equipment deliver tailored workouts based on that

## The idea in one sentence
Latest version first. Keep older versions to show how the idea changed.
- 2026-10-09 (D1, scope changed): Take a photo of the home equipment you own, and get an evidence-based training plan for a set number of weeks, with progression, that combines all of that equipment.
- 2026-10-09 (D0): Take a photo of the home gym equipment you already own, and get one workout that uses exactly that equipment (photo in, workout out).

## Assumptions (ranked by risk)
1. **[RISKIEST, provisional]** A real group of people has unused home equipment, wants to train again, and is stopped by *not having a plan that combines their equipment over weeks* (planning problem), not by motivation or habit. Untested: nobody observed. Counter-evidence: during COVID, equipment-specific workouts were everywhere and people still stopped.
2. A photo is better than typing "I have 5 kg dumbbells and a bench" into ChatGPT or another AI agent.

## Red flags
- **Solution first, problem later** (D0, [MCALLISTER] [KRIEGER]): idea came from a photo-to-poem app, not from a customer or own problem. Founder has not had the problem and knows nobody who has. Rachleff accepts capability-first, but then "who cares?" needs real evidence.
- **Two problems mixed** (D0/D1): "can't find a matching workout" (search) vs "equipment sits in a corner" (motivation). A workout generator solves only the first one.
- **Scope moved during the interview** (D0→D1): from "photo in, one workout out" to a multi-week program with progression. The product is not yet stable in the founder's head.
- **Possible second problem, not first** (D1, [WEINSTEIN]): the COVID story (equipment used twice, then stopped) points to motivation/habit as the first problem. A plan does not fix that.
- **AI agents as direct alternative** (D3, noted): founder named "an agent" as what people use today. To check in D3.
